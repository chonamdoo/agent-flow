## Evidence audit

Check high-impact claims before polishing sentences.

- Does the subject of every number match the source?
- Is a historical benchmark being shown as the current result?
- Is an internal or automated evaluation being presented as user evidence?
- Is a target customer described as confirmed demand without behavioral data?
- Is a competitor claim current, dated, and sourced?
- Is legal or contractual safety claimed beyond the supplied agreement?
- Is an estimate presented as a measurement?
- Is a proposal written as an approved decision?
- Does the conclusion exceed the evidence in the body?

When evidence is missing, do one of the following:

1. Narrow the claim.
2. Label it as an assumption or proposal.
3. Add a specific verification task.
4. Remove it if it does not help the reader.

Never repair missing evidence by inventing a plausible detail.

## Korean writing checks

Prefer natural Korean order and direct business wording.

- Replace unnecessary "~을 가지고 있다" with a direct predicate.
- Remove false passives such as "~되어진다".
- Use "~라고 볼 수 있다" only when real uncertainty remains.
- Reduce repeated sentence-openers such as "또한", "게다가", and "한편".
- Replace empty weight such as "주목할 만하다", "시사하는 바가 크다", and "의미가 있다" with the actual result.
- Replace vague technical nouns with the screen, action, file, behavior, or decision the reader can inspect.
- Avoid compressed noun chains when a short sentence is clearer.
- Keep domain terminology when precision matters, and explain it at first use for mixed audiences.
- Use short declarative sentences in reports, but vary length enough to avoid mechanical rhythm.

Avoid promotional certainty:

- "유일한 합법 구조" becomes a sourced and dated comparison or a verification task.
- "사업화 가능한 수준" becomes the exact technical result plus the business evidence still required.
- "기술 리스크 해소" becomes the specific technical risk tested and the risks not tested.
- "높은 지불 의향" becomes measured conversion data or an explicit customer hypothesis.

## English writing checks

- Replace significance inflation with the concrete event and local impact.
- Replace vague attribution with a named supplied source or narrower claim.
- Remove appended "-ing" phrases that only simulate analysis.
- Prefer "is", "has", or a precise active verb over "serves as", "stands as", and "boasts".
- Keep "not only X but Y" only when the contrast matters.
- Do not force groups of three.
- Repeat the clearest noun instead of cycling through synonyms.
- Remove chatbot framing, generic praise, filler, stacked hedging, and optimistic closing language.
- Use sentence case for headings unless a style guide requires otherwise.

## Structure and typography

- Keep headings that help a reader predict the section content.
- Remove subtitles that merely repeat the title.
- Use tables for exact comparisons and repeated fields.
- Use lists for scan-friendly actions, assumptions, or risks.
- Use prose when every bullet is a decorative label followed by one sentence.
- Use cards only for genuinely parallel summary metrics.
- Keep emphasis selective. Do not bold every key noun.
- Remove decorative emoji unless it carries product meaning or matches the requested voice.
- Preserve required formatting and source links.

## House-style restrictions

Apply punctuation and typography restrictions only when the user, repository, organization, or selected profile requires them. Do not treat one person's preferences as universal writing rules.

For Ricky's clean-writing profile:

- Avoid U+2014 em dash in revised prose.
- Avoid U+00B7 middle dot in revised prose.
- Prefer straight quotation marks unless the destination style requires locale typography.
- Do not alter a verbatim quote, code fragment, identifier, or URL merely to satisfy a punctuation restriction. Separate the verbatim material or explain the conflict.
